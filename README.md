# From-Discovery-to-Handover-NCS-Core-Practice-Questions-and-a-Services-Study-Guide
The Nutanix Certified Services - Core exam focuses on the work required to deliver and expand a Nutanix Cloud Infrastructure cluster for a customer. The NCS-Core 7 Beta blueprint organizes that work into three phases: pre-delivery, delivery, and post-delivery. A useful study plan follows the service engagement from the first scope review through the final handover.

## Exam introduction and overview

The Nutanix blueprint reviewed for this article describes the **NCS-Core 7 Beta** exam in English, with 108 multiple-choice and multiple-response items and 180 minutes. It does not publish a fixed passing score or percentage weight for any domain. Beta logistics and availability can change; check Nutanix's current certification page before scheduling.

The credential concerns implementation services. It is relevant to practitioners who plan customer deployment work, install and configure clusters, verify the result, troubleshoot issues, and transfer operational knowledge.

## Official exam objectives

### Pre-delivery

The blueprint begins with reviewing the service kit, stakeholders, scope, schedule, and bill of materials. Candidates should conduct discovery, confirm customer requirements and constraints, and check both physical site and infrastructure readiness. Preparation may include installation binaries, hardware, Foundation, and remote access. A practical question here is whether the engagement is ready to start, not simply whether equipment has arrived.

### Delivery

Delivery covers hardware installation according to the field guide, deployment with Foundation, verification of Prism access, and NCC checks. It continues through Prism, hypervisor, and management-plane configuration, plus review of LCM and hardening. The blueprint also covers basic services, fit checks, VM migration, cluster conversion, and expansion.

The final delivery steps are testing against customer requirements, recording evidence, troubleshooting, escalating when needed, and retesting. These tasks form a sequence: a configuration is not finished until it is checked and its result can be explained.

### Post-delivery

The blueprint closes with updated as-built documentation and diagrams. The service provider also demonstrates operations, support resources, and case handling during knowledge transfer. Handover is part of delivery quality because the customer needs a usable record of the installed environment.

## A practical preparation routine

Build one deployment scenario and write down the inputs and outputs for each phase. For pre-delivery, list evidence of site readiness. For delivery, record what each Foundation, Prism, and NCC check confirms. For post-delivery, draft the as-built items and a short knowledge-transfer agenda. If a check fails, document the troubleshooting and retest path.

## Using NCS-Core Practice Questions

CertQuestionsBank [NCS-Core practice questions](https://www.certquestionsbank.com/NCS-Core.html) can support structured review of these decisions. Read the explanations and classify errors by phase: discovery and readiness, installation and verification, troubleshooting, or handover. Then return to the Nutanix blueprint and a hands-on lab. These are independent practice materials, not official Nutanix exam items.

## Six preparation tips

1. Learn the full three-phase workflow before drilling isolated product features.
2. Practice identifying missing prerequisites during discovery.
3. Explain what Foundation, Prism, and NCC each contribute to verification.
4. Include evidence and retesting in every troubleshooting scenario.
5. Prepare as-built documentation and knowledge transfer, not only installation steps.
6. Confirm the current exam version and logistics with Nutanix before booking.

## Why the credential matters

NCS-Core addresses the ability to deliver a cluster as a customer service engagement, not just configure a product in isolation. Use the Nutanix NCS-Core 7 Beta Exam Blueprint Guide as the scope reference, and use practice questions to locate gaps in the workflow.
